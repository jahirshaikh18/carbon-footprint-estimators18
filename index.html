🗣️ How to Present This Application to Your Teacher (Demonstration Script)

Follow this 4-step sequence when your ma'am asks: "Show me how your project works person-wise and explain the calculations."

Step 1: Introduction of the Problem & Architecture

"Good morning/afternoon Ma'am. Our project is titled 'Carbon Footprint Estimator'. The goal of this application is to quantify personal greenhouse gas emissions ($CO_2e$) across 4 critical lifestyle sectors: Transportation, Electricity & Energy, Diet, and Waste. It enables us to model different personas—such as a student, a daily metro commuter, or a car-driving professional—to observe how lifestyle choices directly influence our ecological footprint."

Step 2: Demonstrate Person-Wise Variation (The Core Requirement)

"Ma'am, to show how it functions person-wise, we have built-in comparative profiles:
1. Aarav (Student): Travels by bus and metro, consumes ~40 kWh electricity, eats vegetarian meals. His footprint is only ~2.3 tonnes/year.
2. Vikram (Corporate Manager): Drives a private car 700 km/month, runs high AC electricity (320 kWh), and has a meat-heavy diet. His footprint jumps to ~8.8 tonnes/year—almost 4 times higher!
We can also add any new person on the spot by clicking '+ Add New Person Profile'."

Step 3: Explain the Mathematical Formulas & Emission Factors

"The mathematical core uses standard IPCC and Central Electricity Authority (CEA) emission factors:
• Car: Distance (km) × 0.18 kg CO₂e/km
• Two-Wheeler: Distance (km) × 0.07 kg CO₂e/km
• Public Transit: Distance (km) × 0.04 kg CO₂e/km
• Grid Electricity: Energy (kWh) × 0.70 kg CO₂e/kWh
• LPG: Cylinders × 42.5 kg CO₂e/cylinder
• Diet & Waste: Lifecycle assessment coefficients based on standard dietary studies.
The sum is normalized into Monthly kg $CO_2e$ and multiplied by 12 for Annual Tonnes $CO_2e$."

Step 4: Show the 'What-If' Simulation & Action Plan

"Finally Ma'am, an estimator is only useful if it helps people improve. In our 'What-If Simulator', if Vikram replaces just 50% of his car trips with the metro, his emissions reduce by over 1,500 kg $CO_2e$ annually. This proves how actionable individual data is."

❓ Top 6 Viva / Exam Questions & Model Answers

Read through these to be 100% prepared for questions your teacher might ask

Q1: What does "$CO_2e$" mean and why don't we just measure $CO_2$? ▼
Answer: $CO_2e$ stands for Carbon Dioxide Equivalent. Human activities emit multiple greenhouse gases including Methane ($CH_4$), Nitrous Oxide ($N_2O$), and Fluorinated gases, which have vastly higher Global Warming Potentials (GWPs) than carbon dioxide. For example, Methane has a GWP of ~28 over 100 years. Expressing everything in "$CO_2e$" normalizes all gases to an equivalent warming impact of 1 unit of $CO_2$, enabling a single consolidated metric.
Q2: Why does diet impact carbon footprint so significantly? ▼
Answer: Food production accounts for over 25% of global GHG emissions. Livestock, particularly cattle and sheep, produce substantial enteric fermentation (methane gas). Additionally, animal feed production requires immense land clearing, fertilizers ($N_2O$ emissions), and refrigeration energy. Therefore, plant-based diets inherently produce far less GHG per calorie than meat-centric diets.
Q3: How is this application structured and how is user state managed? ▼
Answer: The application follows a reactive frontend architecture. The state contains an array of person profiles, each with activity attributes. When any input or slider changes, the mathematical calculation engine recalculates sector totals, updates the SVG donut chart and DOM metrics, and saves the active profile in browser localStorage for persistent retrieval without needing an external database setup.
Q4: How does the application calculate tree offset requirements? ▼
Answer: An average mature tropical tree absorbs approximately 20 to 22 kg of $CO_2$ per year. Therefore, to neutralize 1 tonne (1,000 kg) of annual $CO_2e$, approximately $1000 / 22 \approx 45\text{ to }50\text{ trees}$ need to be planted and nurtured for a full year.
Q5: What are the limitations of this model and future scopes? ▼
Answer: The current model uses simplified average emission factors. Future scope includes integrating real-time smart meter APIs, GPS-based transit tracking, regional emission factor databases based on user geolocation (e.g. state-specific electrical grid mixes), and cloud-synced multi-user organizational footprints.
